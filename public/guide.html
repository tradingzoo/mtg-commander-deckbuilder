How to review a list

  1. Read the commander's text, every word. One creator lost half a build by misreading Henzie (blitz only works on mana value 4+). List its keywords.
  2. Check the skeleton counts below. They're a floor, not the point.
  3. Check the curve against the commander's mana value (DefCat's 1 → 3 → 5 rule).
  4. Check the win cons. Goldfish to turn 7 or 8 and ask: “Could I win from here? If not, which card in the deck would win if I drew it now?” If no card would, that's the gap to search for. Aim for 3-4 real finishers.
  5. Run the fun lens: cards that hit several synergies, using a card “wrong”, and one card replacing two.
  6. Search for upgrades with the recipes below. Prefer cheap cards that do 2+ jobs and sit under 5% on EDHREC.
  7. Suggest swaps in pairs (out → in), with one line of reason each.

How many copies of an effect you need

About 12 to see it in the first few turns, 8 by turn 6, 5-6 by turn 10. (Based Deck Department)

Mana value rubric

6+ must swing the game the turn it lands. 5: a big advantage right away, or a threat that runs away with the game. 4 pushes the deck into overdrive. 1-3 are engines that keep paying out. Run only 3-4 cards at the top of the curve. (Commander Challenge)

Curve around the commander

With a 5-mana commander: 1-mana ramp on turn 1, 3-mana ramp on turn 2, commander on turn 3, so 2-mana rocks are the weak slot. With a 3-mana commander: no 2-mana rocks, only 1-mana ramp, and cut 3-drops weaker than the commander. Ramp goes 1 → 3 → 5 or 2 → 4 → 6. (DefCat)

The fun lens

Break the commander into keywords

Underline every verb and noun on the card. Look for cards that hit two or more of them and also fit each other. A good deck can win without its commander, and you're still finding new interactions after 30 games. (Commander Challenge)

Use a card wrong

Spend 10 minutes with pencil and paper and write down every way the mechanic could be used, not just the obvious one. Then search Scryfall: “does this exist?” Reverse landfall and an Ad Nauseam deck full of 8-drops came from this. (DefCat)

Skip the third card

If the plan needs commander + X + Y, look for one card that does X and Y. These “secret commanders” broaden the plan and free slots for ramp and interaction. (DefCat)

Pick the lesser-played version

List every card with the effect and favour the obscure ones. Search the trigger wording, then search again with the other way it's printed. (Based Deck Department)

Hide your power

Obscure cards are protection, because nobody knows to kill them. Start slow and be the “secret archenemy.” Commander Challenge's resilient deck won 9 of 17 games (53%). (Commander Challenge)

Resilience and instant speed

Layer 2-3 kinds of resilience into the theme. Red is fragile, so prefer artifacts and enchantments over creatures, run wheels to refill, and keep instant-speed plays. Decide at the end step before your turn. (Commander Challenge)

Every card on plan

Even “generic” ramp, draw and removal should come in the theme's form. Cards that hit 2-4 pillars (draw, mana, interaction, plan) are never dead draws. Have a turn-by-turn plan. (Deck Driver)

EDHREC

  • Themes give separate average decks, e.g. Dáin equipment, burn, extra combats, Treasure.
  • High Synergy and High Lift sections find the odd fits. Cards at 5-10% with positive synergy are the sweet spot: proven, but rarely seen.
  • Under 5%: read individual decks in the Decks tab, filtered by theme, price or bracket.
  • EDHREC Recs: paste your whole list and get suggestions based on every card in it.
  • Use the plain averages only for vanilla staples (removal, draw), not for the fun slots.

Moxfield / Archidekt

Use Moxfield deck search or Archidekt deck search. Filter by commander plus one odd card you like, and see what else those builders paired it with. Drag cards from Scryfall or EDHREC into a “Considering” board, and note why for each.

Combos

Commander Spellbook: search by commander or by card. Bracket 3 allows late two-card combos only, and you should tell the pod.

Worked example: Dáin

Keywords: attacks · Dwarves you control · damage (noncombat, dealt by Dáin) · each opponent · vigilance · haste · mana value 5.

Skip the third card: Dáin needs Dáin + lots of Dwarves + an attack. An Unexpected Party, The Lonely Mountain, Dwarven Mine and Fíli are “lots of Dwarves” in one card (you have them). Lwaxana is the other kind: she doubles the payoff, so you need half the Dwarves.

Curve check: Sol Ring / Mox Amber / Mox Opal on turn 1 (you have all three), a 3-mana rock on turn 2, Dáin on turn 3. That makes Talisman of Conviction, The Mind Stone and Boros Signet the slot to question. The old tests flagged Boros Signet as weak too.
